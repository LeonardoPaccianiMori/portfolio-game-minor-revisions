# Stage 3 — Critique and Recommendations

---

## 1. Direct verdict

**Does *Minor Revisions* currently look like a good game design on its own terms?**

Yes, with one large qualification. It is a good *systems and narrative* design and an incomplete *play* design. Across eleven documents it converts its theme into mechanism with unusual completeness — the claim triad, the two-layer result, the unbending calendar, unscored undetectable misconduct, the trust-gated shortcut, the deflationary endings. Almost nothing in it is decorative. But the layer between the player's hands and those systems — what a station view actually is, what the manuscript board actually does, what a corridor walk actually contains — has never been designed. It has only been constrained. The design knows in precise detail what the player will *decide* and almost nothing about what the player will *do*.

That is a real deficiency, not a documentation lag. The roadmap in `00-design-index.md` runs B00–B10 and contains no block for moment-to-moment interaction feel. B04 ("Laboratory and manuscript gameplay") specifies structure, costs, and prohibitions; B08 specifies presentation limits. Neither asks what the repeated action feels like the fortieth time.

**Does it have a unique and easily identifiable identity?** Yes, unambiguously. See §2.

**Does it support a credible fun hypothesis?** Partly. Credible at the campaign scale, credible at the reading and triage layer, **not yet credible at the moment-to-moment layer**, and unresolved in the middle acts. See §3.

**Are its narrative, artistic, and audio directions clear?** Narrative: yes, exceptionally. Artistic: yes, except for one unresolved contradiction about where absurdity lives. Audio: yes as a plan, with the highest execution variance in the project. See §4–5.

**Do those directions match the expectations?** Very closely — with two exceptions. Pillar 6 ("the game must remain fun and absurdist throughout") is the one stated obligation the design has given itself no method to test. And `README`/`01`'s promise of an "increasingly surreal" institution is forbidden by `09`'s own prohibitions. See §6.

**What cannot be known before a playable slice?** Less than the corpus implies. Several questions currently treated as prototype questions are actually arithmetic or authoring questions that can be closed on paper today — chiefly whether the 64-period budget closes. See §11 for the genuine prototype set.

## 2. Identity verdict

The identity is clear, distinctive, and deliberate. Stage 2 confirmed via `decision-log.md` that every element Stage 1 found distinctive was an explicit dated decision, not an accident.

The identity is: **a survival game about rhetorical scope.** The scarce resources are periods, energy, and defensibility; the terminal act is choosing how strongly to claim; the antagonist is a process with no villain in it; and the game refuses to score the player's ethics. The three sharpest components:

1. **Claim level as the central resource** (`04` "Results and claims"; `12` `manuscript.claim.*`). It is simultaneously wording, PI confidence, integrity risk, reviewer difficulty, and one of three PIIM response cards (`07` "PIIM response band"). I know of very few designs that make rhetorical scope the primary currency.
2. **Undetectable, unpunished, unscored misconduct** (`03`; `07`; `08` "B05 route-unlock contract"; `12` `scene.response.stronger.*`). Detection is gated on the player's own visible record; hidden integrity problems block neither route; fabricated work can publish. This is the design's most valuable property and its greatest polarization risk.
3. **Institutional Citations** (`08`; `12` `MR-CIT-01`–`12`). The achievement system is written in the institution's own voice and is part of the satire rather than a metagame bolted onto it.

Two identity elements are currently weaker than the documents imply:

- **The working-trust system is generic** (five 0–100 bars, ±10/±20, five bands). Its distinctive parts are the permanent flags and Gabriel's pass-through, not the bars themselves. See HGA-R20 and HGA-R21.
- **Two Elena meters** (PI confidence and Elena working trust) both gate Aldercroft, both respond partly to the same behaviours, and are nowhere distinguished for the player. That is complexity not earning a player-facing result. See HGA-R21.

## 3. Fun-hypothesis verdict

I separate this by scale, preserving Stage 1's assessments.

**Moment-to-moment: not yet credible.** The reliable pleasure is *reading* — environmental notices, journal rejections, reviewer reports, Elena's dialogue. `12`'s draft proves that pleasure exists. The unreliable pleasure is *doing*. Strip the fiction and the repeated action is: walk 5–20 m, open a station view, select from ≤3 qualitative options, read a cost preview, confirm, walk away. Sixty-plus times. Two design decisions produce this and both are defensible in isolation — `01` pillar 1 and its dexterity non-goal remove motor skill deliberately, and `10` removes drag/hold/timed/precision input for accessibility. But nothing was put in their place, and no document treats that as a question. This is the design's principal fun risk and it is a *missing decision*, not a weak one.

**Short loop (1–2 periods): credible in Weeks 1–5 and 10–13.** The asynchronous three-slot structure is genuinely well built. `02`'s "waiting creates competing decisions rather than dead time" is delivered by real mechanism: monitoring points force returns, the desk queue cannot operate equipment, windows expire, and night periods carry an energy surcharge. A Week-3 slice has a real decision shape.

**Short loop: a structural gap in Weeks 8–9.** Every experiment window in `12` has closed (laser W1–4, range W2–4, batch W3–6, repair W4–6) and oxygen-loss does not open until W10. Weeks 8–9 contain the preprint commit, three rejections, the research plan, and Camila's first contact. Narratively dense; mechanically empty. See HGA-R07.

**Campaign: credible, and the design's strongest fun engine.** Stage 1's arithmetic — roughly 70–80 periods of demand against 64 available — is exactly the tension `07` describes ("A player risks a crash by trying to maximize evidence, PI confidence, relationships, and both career routes in one run"). Triage among five competing goods is the right engine for this theme. But **the arithmetic has never been done**, three inputs are undefined (monitoring points per run, the protected break's action class, diminishing returns on repeats), and the whole thesis fails in either direction if the budget is loose or impossible. See HGA-R04.

**Learnability: currently contradicted by its own mechanism.** `01` pillar 2 promises intelligible causes; `02` and `07` promise the player can "learn causal patterns." The mechanism is a three-band table (80/20/0 · 20/60/20 · 0/20/80) observed across ~8–12 total resolutions. At that sample size a Mixed-band player cannot distinguish preparation quality from variance. This is a weak decision, cheaply fixable by display rather than by re-tuning. See HGA-R05.

**A genuine unintended incentive.** `02`/`07`'s break economics — first protected break in a week restores two segments, later ones restore one — teach every player to front-load one break per week and avoid later ones. The result is a uniform weekly rhythm rather than the crisis-driven recovery the crash system implies. Folded into HGA-R04.

## 4. Narrative-direction verdict

**Clear and strong.** This is the most complete part of the project. `03`'s beat sheet plus `12`'s scripts specify not only events but tone, and the arc (euphoria → clarification → inflation → exposure → negotiation → attrition → departure) matches `01`'s stated arc exactly.

Three structural achievements deserve naming because they are theme-as-mechanism rather than theme-as-text:

- **The Week 6–7 pair.** Elena removes cautious wording, then asks for it back, and the paper returns near its original form: "Yes. But now it has been revised by history" (`12` `scene.whatWeHad.elena.answer`). That is the whole game in two beats.
- **Reviewer 2 versus Reviewer 3 with Editor Haddad asking for both** (`03`; `12` `record.reviewer2`, `record.reviewer3`, `record.piimEditor`). The impossibility is structural, not authorial complaint.
- **Morrow opens because academia rejects you.** `MR-OPT-CAMILA-INITIAL` is gated on the *Knowledge* rejection. The industry route is a consequence of failure, not a reward for success.

Four weaknesses:

1. **Non-nihilism rests on very little.** `01` forbids nihilism and names solidarity as the counterweight. Concretely the counterweight is one relationship afterbeat per run, of which five of fifteen modules are `*-SUPPORT`, plus `MR-CIT-05`. All four career endings are deflationary; the warmest line in the corpus is `ending.career.leave`. Whether the game lands "bitter but human" or simply bleak currently depends on a handful of strings. See HGA-R11.
2. **Camila carries an entire alternative life in three emails.** `05` and `09` bar her from a model or on-floor presence; `12` gives her `MR-OPT-CAMILA-INITIAL`, `-VIDEO`, `-OFFER`. Pillar 4 ("the exit is meaningful, not utopian") depends on her. See HGA-R12.
3. **The Week-6 contradictory-revision beat has no content object**, despite `03` requiring it and `MR-SCN-WHAT-WE-HAD` depending on it. See HGA-R15.
4. **Complicity may not reach most players.** `01`'s middle stage is "uncomfortable complicity," but the three integrity acts are explicit, labelled, single-confirmation choices with stated costs. If the honest path is comfortably completable, most players will end the game uncomplicit and the arc's middle stage will not fire. See HGA-R09.

## 5. Artistic and audio-direction verdict

**Artistic: clear, with one contradiction.** `09` defines stylized institutional realism positively and negatively, specifies nine palette roles with functions and non-colour redundancy, and makes the sharpest presentation decision in the project: *the organoid and its data displays are the only visibly advanced things in the world.* That single contrast carries the satire visually. The asset limits (one kit, ≤24 prop families, 4 NPCs, 1K textures, ≤75 MB) are coherent with the €150 ceiling.

The contradiction: `README` says the institution becomes "increasingly surreal" and `01` pillar 2 says "increasingly absurd," while `09` bans distorted architecture, giant props, slapstick, and glitch effects, and `06` caps floor change at one major and two minor items per phase across the entire 400 m². The approved act-state progression is entirely realistic: booking pressure → revision piles → preprint traces → colder light → emptier rooms. **The design currently delivers escalating institutional realism, not surrealism.** Textual-only surrealism is a legitimate and probably correct direction; the problem is that two top-level documents describe a different game. See HGA-R13.

Secondary artistic concern: twenty lighting presets (5 act states × 4 periods) must convey sixteen weeks of decay in five discrete steps. Partly addressed by HGA-R13.

**Audio: clear as a plan, highest execution variance in the project.** The non-lexical vocal palette (5 characters × 8 sounds, named roles including Gabriel's "queue-warning sigh" and Camila's "call-end tone") is a genuine aesthetic and a correct economy under E14. It is also the single easiest thing in the corpus to make grating: eight sounds per character across ~17 scenes plus message beats, with only "controlled pitch and rhythm variation" as mitigation. Six music stems with a no-constant-score policy is sufficient and means the eight room ambiences are the dominant sonic experience — which is consistent with the theme. `MR-MUS-04` "Public record: bright institutional swell" is a correctly satirical brief.

## 7. Strongest existing design commitments

These are load-bearing and correct. Recommendations below are constrained by them.

1. **Theme-to-mechanism conversion.** Gabriel's trust value drives a level-design shortcut (`06`), scene availability (`12`), a support rule (`05`, `07`), and an epilogue afterbeat (`08`). That cross-system load-bearing is the signature of a designed game.
2. **Two-layer results** (`04`, `07`). Encodes the distinction between "the tissue did something" and "I can defend it," and makes failure informative by construction.
3. **Fair-play architecture** (`07`, `11`, `02`). Seed locked at start, no reroll on reload, no real-time advance, windows only pass on deliberate advance after warning, crashes barred during manual actions and cutscenes.
4. **Detection gated on the player's own record** (`03`, `05`, `12`). No omniscient watchdog. Concealment becomes a skill and paranoia becomes a real emotion.
5. **No moral score anywhere** (`05`, `07`, `08`, `10`, `12`).
6. **Closed content boundary** (`12`: 6/7/10/20/29/12/30, ≤6,000 unique words, exact fallback cut line, `MR-TEST-CONT-001`). Scope is enumerated, not aspirational.
7. **The exit's authored appeal from Week 8 for every player** (`06`, `09`).
8. **The 06:42 bookend** (`03`).
9. **Elena as non-villain with her own panel** (`05`, `12` `MR-OPT-ELENA-FUTURE`).
10. **Accessibility as design value rather than compliance**, reinforced rather than opposed by the dexterity rejection.

## 8. Critical recommendations

### HGA-R01 — Specify the manuscript board as a system before any work package touches it
**CRITICAL · DOCUMENT-SUPPORTED**

**Problem.** The board is where the game's central verb lives and it has no specified behaviour. `07` "Manuscript system" names seven card types (figure, evidence, claim, control, authorship, request, supplementary), a requirements panel, and commit snapshots. `12` supplies three claim strings and three requirements strings. Nothing states how many cards attach where, what conflicts, or what the player is solving. Under `15`'s definition of done item 1 ("its approved behaviour and failure path are implemented") and its source-of-truth order, an implementation worker on `MR-WP-05`/`MR-WP-08` would have to escalate rather than build. This is a *missing decision*, and it sits at the centre of Weeks 5–8 and Week 14.

**Sources.** `07` "Manuscript system"; `12` `manuscript.requirements.*`, `MR-TASK-MANUSCRIPT`, `MR-SCN-WHAT-WE-HAD`, `MR-SCN-A-REASONABLE-RESPONSE`; `15` `MR-REQ-UI-001`, `MR-WP-05`, `MR-WP-08`.

**Likely player effect if unresolved.** Weeks 5–8 — a fifth of the campaign and the entire "Manuscript hell" act — degrade into clicking a claim toggle and a Commit button. The three integrity acts (`scene.response.stronger.omit/.alter/.fabricate`) become menu items rather than physical removals of something a claim needed. The satire's participatory dimension is lost: the player watches inflation happen rather than performing it.

**Smallest change.** Specify the board as a small constraint-satisfaction surface with a fixed vocabulary, using only existing card types: each claim level declares a required number of support slots by card type (careful < strong < inflated support burden is *inverted* — the inflated claim requires the *most* support and therefore visibly lacks it); the requirements panel enumerates unmet slots and conflicts in plain text; committing with unmet slots is permitted and recorded. Omitting a valid card physically empties a slot a claim depends on. No typing, no new card types, no new UI class.

**Preserves.** `07`'s card-based no-typing board, requirements panel, commit snapshots without free undo, and the careful/strong/inflated triad; `01`'s non-goal against simulated word processing; `10`'s no-drag/no-timed input rule (slots are selection-and-confirm).

**Risks and costs.** A constraint puzzle can become fiddly or can make the inflated claim *mechanically* punished, which would violate `07`'s no-moral-score principle — the slot deficit must read as "unsupported," not as "wrong." Adds one authoring pass over the evidence-card tag vocabulary.

**Affected.** `07` (manuscript system), `12` (`manuscript.*` strings and requirements text), `10` (station-view spec), `15` (`MR-REQ-UI-001` acceptance criteria).

**Minimum test.** Paper test first: enumerate every claim level × packet band (Thin/Developing/Coherent/Substantial) and confirm each combination produces a distinct, legible requirements-panel state and at least one non-trivial choice. Then include the board in the vertical slice as `12` already plans, and confirm Leonardo can articulate, unprompted after play, why one arrangement is weaker than another.

### HGA-R02 — Make "fun and absurdist throughout" a testable criterion with a named owner
**CRITICAL · DOCUMENT-SUPPORTED**

**Problem.** `01` pillar 6 and decision log 2026-08-27 make engagement a positive obligation across the whole runtime; `07` restates it ("escalation, not repetitive grinding"). `13` contains twelve test IDs, twelve slice-completion items, and eight experience criteria — and no engagement, pacing, or repetition check. Seven of the eight criteria are comprehension or meaning criteria; the eighth covers humour. The approved evaluation apparatus would pass a game that is legible, meaningful, funny, well-tested, accessible, private, and tedious. `00`'s roadmap has no block that ever owned this question.

**Sources.** `01` pillar 6 and "Experience success criteria"; `07` "Balance principles"; `13` all test IDs and "Vertical-slice evaluation" items 1–6; `00` roadmap B00–B10.

**Likely player effect if unresolved.** The design's largest risk — Stage 1's F1–F4 (traversal tax, confirm-dialogue fatigue, Weeks 8–9 vacuum, analysis-view decay) — reaches release unmeasured, because nothing was ever asked to measure it.

**Smallest change.** Add a ninth experience criterion and a matching slice-gate item, both **pre-registered**: before playing the slice, Leonardo writes down thresholds he will accept — e.g. maximum acceptable share of session time in traversal; maximum confirm-dialogue count per minute; whether he voluntarily continues past the slice's end. Instrument the slice to log station visits, traversal seconds, and confirmations per minute so the judgement is made against numbers he committed to in advance rather than against post-hoc feeling. This uses only Leonardo and Codex.

**Preserves.** E13's sole-evaluator boundary and `13`'s refusal to recruit participants; `01`'s qualitative-criteria approach (this adds one criterion in the same register).

**Risks and costs.** Self-evaluation remains biased; pre-registration reduces but does not remove that. Adds a small instrumentation task to `MR-WP-07`, which must be development-only and must not become telemetry (P9, `11`).

**Affected.** `01` ("Experience success criteria"), `13` (test IDs, slice gate, full-game gate), `00` (roadmap gate criteria), `15` (`MR-REQ-TEST-001`).

**Minimum test.** The criterion is the test. Its validity check is whether the pre-registered thresholds can be stated in one page before the slice exists.

### HGA-R03 — Resolve the comprehension criteria that the sole-evaluator rule cannot verify
**CRITICAL · DOCUMENT-SUPPORTED**

**Problem.** `01`'s criterion 1 is "A player without academic experience can explain the main objective"; criterion 2 concerns whether each experiment's purpose is understandable. `13` forbids recruiting external players and names Leonardo the only human evaluator, and decision log 2026-08-23 records that the material derives from his own experience. He cannot proxy for a non-academic reader. At least criteria 1, 2, and arguably 4, 5, and 8 are structurally unverifiable under the approved method. `13` acknowledges an adjacent limit ("not a claim about a general audience") without resolving the dependency.

**Sources.** `01` "Experience success criteria" 1–2; `13` "Evaluation boundary" and "Fallback and full-game evaluation"; decision log 2026-08-23 and 2026-08-28.

**Likely player effect if unresolved.** E5 — the design's most audience-facing promise — ships on assumption. If the science, the evidence views, or the PIIM card logic are opaque to a non-academic reader, no approved process would discover it.

**Smallest change.** Add a **cold-read protocol** to `13`, executed by Codex in a session with no access to the design documents and no prior context: given only `strings.en.json` (or the `12` draft) plus screenshots of the three evidence views and the Research Status panel, state (a) the protagonist's objective, (b) why the laser/sham task matters, (c) what the repatterning index means, (d) what happens if evidence stays Thin. Repeat once against the built slice. Record pass or a specific rewrite request per item. Then reword `01`'s criteria to say what this method can actually claim: comprehensibility to a reader without the design documents, not to a general audience.

**Preserves.** E13 exactly — no participants, no data collection, no consent, no surveys, only Leonardo and Codex; and P1's discipline of not overclaiming what the evidence supports.

**Risks and costs.** An LLM cold reader is not a human non-academic and will over-perform on jargon and under-perform on affect. The protocol must therefore be limited to *comprehension*, never to humour or emotional criteria — which means criteria 4, 5, and 8 remain judged by Leonardo alone and should be labelled as such.

**Affected.** `01` ("Experience success criteria"), `13` (new test flow under `MR-TEST-CONT-001` or a new ID), `00` (gate criteria).

**Minimum test.** Run the cold read against the existing `12` draft now, before any code. It costs nothing and will either validate the text or produce a concrete rewrite list.

### HGA-R04 — Close the time and energy economy on paper before implementation
**CRITICAL · DOCUMENT-SUPPORTED**

**Problem.** The campaign's central engine — triage among five competing goods across 64 periods — has never been arithmetically closed, and three inputs are undefined. (a) **Monitoring points per run:** `02` says "a meaningful monitoring point" (singular), `07` says "meaningful points" (plural), and `12` charges per monitor; total demand swings by 10–20 periods on this alone. (b) **The protected break has no `MR-ACT-` ID** despite costing one period, while `02` states "No later agent may add a new time-costing action class without a requirement change" — the energy economy's only recovery mechanism is currently unimplementable. (c) **Diminishing returns on repeats are unquantified** (`07` "Later repeats have diminishing returns"), so the reachability of a Substantial packet — an Aldercroft condition — is unknown. Stage 1's estimate of ~70–80 periods of demand against 64 available may be right, but the design cannot currently tell. Separately, the break economics (first-in-week restores 2, later restore 1) create a degenerate incentive to take exactly one break early each week, flattening the recovery rhythm the crash system implies.

**Sources.** `02` "B10 authored action-cost contract" and the no-new-action-class rule; `07` "Time, pacing, and pressure profiles" (48–52 / 52–56 / 58–62) and "Evidence, PI confidence…"; `12` action table (no break entry); `04` repeat allowance.

**Likely player effect if unresolved.** Two symmetrical failures. If the budget is looser than intended, the game's core tension evaporates and the honest path is trivially completable — which also kills the complicity arc (HGA-R09). If it is tighter than intended, every route becomes a forced crash and `07`'s "No viable route requires a crash" is false.

**Smallest change.** One spreadsheet, no code. Enumerate every `MR-ACT-` ID × count for three archetype runs — minimal-defensible, thorough-honest, maximising — against 64 periods and the Standard energy curve. Fix monitoring points at a single stated number per template. Assign the protected break an action ID (`MR-ACT-BREAK`, 1 period, energy restore, `MR-REQ-LOOP-001`, `MR-TEST-EXP-001`). Quantify diminishing returns (e.g. first repeat +1, later repeats +0). Set the break restore to a flat value unless the weekly-first bonus is deliberately intended to teach weekly rhythm — and if it is, say so.

**Preserves.** Every approved cost, the 64-period calendar, both pressure profiles, the crash rule, and `07`'s post-slice tuning clause (which permits changing only band percentages — this recommendation touches none of them).

**Risks and costs.** May reveal that the 3-hour target and the full content set are incompatible, forcing an early confrontation with `14`'s fallback path. That confrontation is better had now than in month twelve.

**Affected.** `02` (action-cost contract), `07` (period budgets, break rules, repeat returns), `12` (action table, experiment windows), `15` (`MR-REQ-LOOP-001`, `MR-REQ-EXP-002` acceptance criteria).

**Minimum test.** The spreadsheet is the test. Pass condition: a thorough-honest run completes with ≥4 periods of slack and no forced crash; a maximising run overruns by ≥8 periods. Codex can then encode the same arithmetic as a `MR-TEST-EXP-001` fixture.

### HGA-R05 — Make the earned outcome band visible so causality is actually learnable
**CRITICAL · DOCUMENT-SUPPORTED**

**Problem.** `01` pillar 2 promises intelligible causes; `02` states "The player must be able to learn causal patterns even when information is incomplete"; `07` repeats it. The mechanism is `07`'s three-band table — Robust 80/20/0, Mixed 20/60/20, Compromised 0/20/80 — sampled across roughly 8–12 experiment resolutions per campaign. At that sample size the Mixed band is indistinguishable from noise. `07`'s tuning clause permits changing only the three percentages, which cannot fix a sample-size problem. This is a weak decision, not a missing one.

**Sources.** `02` "Confirmed outcome principles"; `07` "Experiment resolution" and "B10 outcome-band and tuning contract"; `04` "B10 authored experiment baselines".

**Likely player effect if unresolved.** A player who prepares carefully and receives a Limited result cannot tell whether they erred or were unlucky. Perceived arbitrariness in a game whose entire subject is the relationship between preparation and defensible evidence. It also silently punishes the honest-careful playstyle the design otherwise rewards.

**Smallest change.** Do not change the bands. Change what is shown. At the start-confirmation step, name the earned band in plain language alongside the existing cost preview: "Preparation: mixed. Result likely limited." After resolution, the existing stated-reason line names the band as the cause: "Mixed preparation; limited result." The player then learns the *deterministic* causal model (inputs → band) with certainty, while the outcome inside the band retains its locked variation. This requires no new system: `02` already mandates "a short plain-language forecast of the likely trade-off" before a meaningful action and `07` already mandates a stated reason after every material change.

**Preserves.** The band table, the stored seed, the no-reroll-on-reload rule, `07`'s prohibition on hiding a player-caused problem, and `10`'s no-hidden-formulas rule (a band name is a label, not a formula).

**Risks and costs.** Reduces surprise and moves the game further toward Stage 2's axis A4 Direction 1 (transparent planning) and away from dread. That is a real tonal cost and should be a conscious choice, not a side effect. An alternative with the same fix cost: make Robust and Compromised fully deterministic (100/0/0 and 0/0/100) and leave variation only in the Mixed band, which reduces noise where it is least informative.

**Affected.** `02` (outcome principles, forecast rule), `07` (resolution and band contract), `10` (station-view display), `12` (forecast/reason strings), `15` (`MR-REQ-EXP-002`).

**Minimum test.** Codex fixture: for each template, assert that band name appears in both the pre-commit forecast and the post-resolution reason, and that identical inputs always produce the identical band. Leonardo's slice check: after three laser/sham runs with different preparation, can he state the causal rule without consulting `07`?

### HGA-R06 — Decide station-view interaction texture, and enrich two stations rather than all six
**CRITICAL · DOCUMENT-SUPPORTED**

**Problem.** No document specifies what a focused station view *is*, beyond: it stops movement, costs nothing to leave, shows one action with its costs, and uses ordinary selection (`02`, `04`, `10`). Six functional stations are visited 60+ times per run. The absence is a missing decision at the exact point where Stage 1 located the game's largest fun risk. `01` pillar 1 and the dexterity non-goal removed motor skill deliberately; nothing was put in its place.

**Sources.** `02` "Station, sample, and feedback rules"; `04` "B04 interaction contract" and "B08 science presentation and access contract"; `06` "B07 functional stations and room objects"; `10` "Supported devices and controls"; `00` roadmap (no block owns interaction feel).

**Likely player effect if unresolved.** The default outcome is Stage 2's axis A1 Direction 1 — a confirmation surface — because that is the cheapest thing to build and nothing forbids it. Sixty confirm dialogues across three hours, in a game satirising administrative burden by means of administrative burden.

**Smallest change.** Choose Direction 2 (diegetic instrument) for exactly **two** stations and keep Direction 1 for the other four. The two: the **active sample rack** in tissue culture, where the physical label and state signal already exist in `06` and `02`; and the **imaging and monitoring bay**, where the three evidence views already exist in `04`. At those two, the state is read off object and readout rather than off a text summary, and the monitoring decision follows the reading rather than preceding it. All input remains selection-and-confirm.

**Preserves.** `01` pillar 1 (no dexterity), `10`'s prohibition on drag/hold/timed/precision input, `04`'s prohibition on operational quantities and real protocols, the two-channel information rule, and Interaction Assist's bounds. It also honours `02`'s stated justification for physical presence, which currently has no mechanical content to justify.

(b) `15`'s `MR-REQ-EXP-001` requires "six templates" unconditionally while the approved fallback ships four; the requirement lacks the fallback qualifier that `MR-REQ-CONTENT-002` has. (c) A blank line splits the decision-log table before the 2026-08-28 B10 rows, breaking the Markdown table in a document that sits fourth in `15`'s authority order. Separately, `09` states both "no more than 24 core reusable 3D prop families" and "twenty reusable prop families" without cross-reference.
**Sources.** `decision-log.md`; `02`; `03`; `12`; `15` `MR-REQ-EXP-001`; `09`.

**Smallest change.** Record the scene-time supersession; add a fallback qualifier to `MR-REQ-EXP-001`; repair the table; add a one-clause note distinguishing the 24 limit from the 20 plan.

### HGA-R24 — Make environmental text readable without a focused view
**OPTIONAL · PROTOTYPE-REQUIRED**

**Problem.** Thirty environmental items exist, cost no time, are one-time-full-text, room- and act-windowed, and have no markers. `10` forbids objective arrows and bounds Interaction Assist. Stage 1 rated these the game's most reliable micro-pleasure and predicted many players will not find them, especially from Week 8 when attention narrows.
**Sources.** `06` "B10 environmental content boundary"; `12` `MR-ENV-*`; `02` (reading costs no period); `10` (Interaction Assist).
**Effect.** The best writing in the game may be missed by players under pressure.
**Smallest change.** Let short environmental items display their full text in the lower-centre interaction prompt rather than requiring a focused view, so reading costs a glance rather than a mode change. Longer items keep the focused view.
**Preserves.** The no-marker rule (no highlight is added), the one-time display rule, the two-channel rule, Interaction Assist's bounds, and `06`'s rule that no required fact depends on environmental text.
**Risks.** Prompt-area clutter; possible conflict with `10`'s rule that the lower-centre prompt shows "one available action." Needs a length cap.
**Affected.** `10`, `06`, `12` (item length constraints).
**Test.** Slice measurement: how many of the Week-1 environmental items Leonardo reads under each scheme, against a pre-registered target.

### HGA-R25 — Make "stop the experiment" legible as triage rather than as a dead option
**OPTIONAL · DOCUMENT-SUPPORTED**

**Problem.** The monitoring choice set is continue / quality-check-or-stabilize / stop. Stopping loses the group and the elapsed time, while raw records are permanent anyway and weak records still yield information and a repeat reason (`04`, `07`). Its only real use is freeing one of three slots when a window is closing — a legitimate triage function that is never presented as such.
**Sources.** `02` "Recurring five-stage experiment loop" step 4; `04` "B04 interaction contract"; `07` "Experiment interaction state".
**Effect.** A three-option decision reads as a two-option decision plus a trap, until a player independently discovers the slot-pressure use.
**Smallest change.** Have the stop confirmation state its actual function — that it frees a slot immediately — alongside its cost, using the existing plain-language forecast mechanism.
**Preserves.** The three-slot limit, the loss-on-stop rule, the permanent raw record, `02`'s pre-action forecast rule.
**Risks.** None material.
**Affected.** `02`, `07`, `12` (forecast/confirmation strings).
**Test.** Included in the slice's monitoring-path fixture (`13` already requires "a limited or missed-monitoring fixture").

### HGA-R26 — Increase internal-thought density within the word budget
**OPTIONAL · DOCUMENT-SUPPORTED**

**Problem.** The protagonist is deliberately light (`05`), has no spoken performance, and appears only as a silhouette. All interiority rests on internal thought, of which the drafted scenes contain typically one to two lines each. `05` permits one short internal thought before an important spoken choice; nothing provides interiority during the ~60 station actions where the player spends most of the game.
**Sources.** `05` "Narrative voice" and "Dialogue principles"; `09` protagonist silhouette boundary; `12` scene drafts.
**Effect.** The character who is losing a career may be less present in the player's experience than the notices on the walls. The internal voice is also the game's second-best comic instrument after institutional language and is currently under-used.
**Smallest change.** Author a small bounded set of internal lines attached to *state thresholds* rather than to locations — first zero-energy push-through, first Compromised integrity state, first missed monitoring window, first night period, first act transition. Perhaps eight to twelve lines total, well inside the 6,000-word cap, one-time each, non-repeating.
**Preserves.** The light-authoring principle, the no-constant-jokes rule in `05`, the ≤2-variant discipline, the no-generated-text rule, the word cap.
**Risks.** Word budget; risk of the "detached narrator who cannot care about other people" that `05` explicitly warns against; must not become a nag channel.
**Affected.** `05`, `12` (new `internal.*` keys and count), `10` (internal-thought text style already specified).
**Test.** Cold read: with the added lines, does the reader describe the protagonist as a person with a stake?

## 12. Elements that should not be normalized or removed

These are the choices most likely to attract "but players expect…" pressure during implementation. Each is coherent with the vision and several are the reason the design is worth building.

1. **Undetectable, unpunished, unscored misconduct.** Fabricated work can publish (`03`, `08`). Do not add a detection roll, a delayed exposure, or a karmic ending.
2. **No moral score, label, or ending rank** (`05`, `07`, `08`, `10`).
3. **The calendar never waits** (`03`, `07`). Do not add a grace week or a catch-up resource.
4. **No game-over before Week 16** (`02`, `03`, `07`).
5. **The involuntary crash with its fair-play carve-outs** (`02`). Do not make it avoidable by a stat purchase or convert it into a fail state.
6. **No quest markers, minimap, objective arrows, or training room** (`02`, `06`, `10`).
7. **No dexterity, timed input, drag, or hold anywhere** (`01`, `10`). HGA-R06 enriches *reading*, not input.
8. **Institutional Citations in the institution's voice, none requiring unethical play** (`08`, `12`).
9. **The 06:42 bookend and the single continuous floor with no home scene** (`03`, `06`).
10. **Elena as a non-villain with her own panel** (`05`, `12` `MR-OPT-ELENA-FUTURE`).
11. **The exit's growing appeal for every player, independent of route** (`06`, `09`).
12. **Text-led dialogue, no voice acting, non-lexical palettes, silent protagonist** (`05`, `09`).
13. **Deterministic seed locked at experiment start; no reroll on reload; no real-time advance** (`07`, `11`).
14. **Local-only, account-free, telemetry-free persistence** (`10`, `11`).
15. **Deflationary endings.** **Pending Appointment** must remain a bridge appointment; **Transferable Skills** must remain imperfect (`08`).
16. **Supported profile with identical content and no stigma** (`02`, `07`, `10`).
17. **The ≤2-variant discipline and the 6,000-word cap** (`03`, `05`, `12`). These are why the writing can be good.
18. **The asset-provenance gate that applies to prototypes** (`09`, `14`, `ASSET_MANIFEST.md`).
19. **The refusal to recruit participants** (`13`). HGA-R02 and HGA-R03 work inside it.
20. **The separation of agent-direction evidence from the fiction** (`01`). It is working; do not soften it.

## 14. Final risk statement

The dominant risk in *Minor Revisions* is not that it will be incoherent, unfair, inaccessible, legally exposed, or thematically confused. The documentation has protected it against all of those with unusual thoroughness, and the decision log shows that protection was built deliberately over eleven blocks.

The dominant risk is that it will be **admirable and inert** — a game whose every system is correct, legible, and thematically earned, and whose repeated physical act is walking twelve metres to press a confirm button. That risk exists because the design process never opened a block on interaction feel, because two of its most important interaction surfaces (the manuscript board and the station view) were never specified, and because the approved evaluation apparatus contains twelve test IDs, twelve slice items, and eight experience criteria that between them cannot detect tedium.

The second risk is arithmetic. The campaign's best engine — triage among five competing goods across 64 periods — has never been computed, and three of its inputs are undefined. If it is loose, the game's tension and its complicity arc both evaporate. If it is impossible, `07`'s promise that no viable route requires a crash is false. Both failures are silent and both are discoverable this week with a spreadsheet.

The third risk is capacity. Roughly 380–620 hours, at €150, must produce a full TypeScript/Three.js codebase across ten work packages, one continuous 400 m² floor, an IndexedDB layer with backup and migration, a semantic accessible UI, twenty prop families, four rigged NPCs, forty original vocal sounds, six music stems, and a test suite at 90% line and 85% branch coverage with Playwright flows in three engines. `14`'s stop, pause, and reframe rules already treat this honestly, and the 90-minute fallback is more likely to be the terminal deliverable than the three-hour target. That is not a failure condition: a coherent 90-minute *Minor Revisions* that delivers its claim triad, its reviewer contradiction, its unscored fabrication, and its 06:42 would be a complete and unusual work.

Against those risks, the project has an asset that most designs at this stage do not: a body of authored text that already proves the tone works, and a documentation discipline whose refusal to let a plan pose as evidence is the same discipline the game's protagonist may fail to keep. That parallel is the most interesting thing in the corpus. It also means the corpus can be trusted when it says what it does not yet know — which is precisely why the six critical recommendations above concern things it has not yet noticed it does not know.
