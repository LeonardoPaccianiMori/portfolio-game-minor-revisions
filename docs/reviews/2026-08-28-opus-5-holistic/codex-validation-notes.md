## Qualifications and overstatements

- The verdict that this is a good design is an expert interpretation, not
  measured play evidence. Fun can only become evidence through a playable
  slice and later private evaluation.
- The claim that moment-to-moment fun is not yet credible is a risk hypothesis.
  The documents cannot measure how laboratory views, walking, reading, or
  manuscript work will feel in play.
- The Stage-3 estimate of roughly 70–80 periods of demand is not reliable until
  the missing action counts and repeat rules are fixed. The supported finding
  is that the current economy is unresolved.
- The claim that the three-band model cannot teach causal patterns is a
  plausible sample-size concern, not a proved player outcome. It needs a slice
  test or a design decision about intended transparency.
- The claim that Weeks 8 and 9 are mechanically empty is too broad. They have
  no open laboratory experiment, but they contain other required and optional
  work.
- The claimed contradiction between an increasingly surreal institution and
  institutional-realism art is interpretive. Institutional rules, text, and
  incentives can become surreal without distorted architecture. Treat this as
  a wording and execution question, not a proven contradiction.
- The claim that non-nihilism rests on one afterbeat is a tonal hypothesis.
  Career, paper, final-image, and relationship modules can interact in ways
  that a document-only review cannot measure.
- The suggested arithmetic pass bands, cold-read protocol, interaction
  variants, and prototype thresholds are Opus proposals. They are not approved
  requirements.
- The production-hour estimate is approximate. It does not replace the
  approved 6–8-hour weekly capacity and 12–18-month planning range.
