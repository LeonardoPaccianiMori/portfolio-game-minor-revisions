# Narrative and Campaign

Status: **seeded**

## Premise

The protagonist enters the final semester of a fixed-term postdoc with genuinely
promising data. Their PI says only a few supplementary data points and a few
more experiments are needed. Publication appears urgent because it may allow
the protagonist to compete for another academic position.

The discovery is real within the fiction. The satire comes from the expanding
definition of sufficient evidence, contradictory incentives, and the mismatch
between the paper's felt urgency and its eventual effect on the protagonist's
life.

## Five-act spine

### Act I — Supplementary data

The player repeats the laser-damage result, performs sham controls, compares
damage and recovery ranges, and checks different tissue batches. The player
then collects repair-state evidence without proving cause. Some experiments
succeed, others fail, and equipment, samples, schedules, and new PI requests
make the supposedly small task expand.

### Act II — Manuscript hell

The PI eventually agrees to write with the available evidence. An interactive
revision board turns contradictory edits into time, coherence, approval, and
integrity trade-offs. Later instructions may undo earlier instructions until
the manuscript resembles its original version.

### Act III — Submission

The player posts a preprint to a fictional public repository and submits to a
journal. Several journals reject rapidly. One eventually sends the manuscript
for peer review.

### Act IV — Competing futures

Reviewers and the editor generate further demands, including physiologically
relevant injury experiments. The oxygen-loss challenge is required for the
paper response. Drug exposure is optional and can strengthen industry interest.
Two reviewers may be enthusiastic while one is scathing; apparently minor
revisions may require major new experiments.

After reading the public preprint, a cardiac organoid or tissue-chip company
contacts the protagonist about a translational R&D or assay-development role.
The industry contact becomes politely more persistent and the offer more
concrete while the PI frames publication as the gateway to another academic
position.

### Act V — Decision and epilogue

When routes are available, the protagonist chooses academic continuation or
industry. Poor play may leave neither route viable. A short modular, bitterly
comic epilogue responds to paper state, integrity, energy, and relationships.

## Confirmed narrative rules

- Publication is possible but not guaranteed and is not an automatic happy
  ending.
- A preprint may support the industry path even without journal publication.
- Industry is an exit from the academic loop, not proof that all problems end.
- Academic continuation is not automatic punishment, but even its strongest
  outcome reveals limited structural security.
- Cutscenes are real-time, skippable, captioned, and safe around save
  checkpoints.
- Earlier history may be communicated through the world, dialogue, and
  accumulated consequences rather than a long prologue.
- The PI can pressure the player to use the inflated term “reconstructive
  rhythmogenesis” and to make a stronger claim than the evidence supports.
- The protagonist can use careful, strong, or inflated manuscript language.
  This choice changes PI confidence, integrity, and later reviewer pressure.
