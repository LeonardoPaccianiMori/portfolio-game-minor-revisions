# 01 — Vision and Experience Promise

Status: **Documented — approved by Leonardo on 2026-09-10 (Block A1); revised
2026-09-13 to state the cross-discipline audience.**

## Working title

**Minor Revisions.** The title is ironic: the revisions are never minor, and the
postdoc's life is the thing being revised. **The Last Author** is recorded as an
alternative to re-evaluate later. A title-conflict check happens before any
public release.

## One-sentence concept

A short, caustic survival game about a junior researcher trying to keep a
contract and a publication alive inside an academic system that consumes the
people it depends on.

## Thesis

Academia rests on the toxic, exploitative labour of junior figures. It works
almost like a pyramid scheme: you climb one step or you are pushed out. The game
laughs at this and refuses to soften the critique. The intended aftertaste is
recognition: "this is terrible, and it is exactly what I went through."

## Experience promise

- **Surface.** Absurd, sarcastic, recognisable comedy about academic life.
- **Middle.** Pressure, complicity, and the discovery that effort does not
  secure safety because the standard keeps moving.
- **End.** Bleak but human. No ending repairs the system; the game is about what
  the player keeps and what they become.

## Audience

The primary audience is people who tried an academic career and left, who will
recognise their experience in the game. Non-academic players, including
academics from other disciplines, must still understand everything required;
insider knowledge rewards recognition but is never necessary.

## Tone and comedy

Sarcastic, caustic, and absurdist. The institution speaks in cheerful
bureaucratic language that contradicts reality: onboarding handbooks,
performance reviews, wellness programmes, forms, and automated emails.
Occasional surprising meta moments are allowed. The player is never mocked for
suffering; the system is the target. Comedy comes first, and the recognition of
what the comedy hides follows.

## Inspirations

- **The Stanley Parable** — meta-game structure and sophisticated humour. We do
  not recreate it.
- **Superliminal** — a distinct identity and playful surprise.
- **Mini Metro** — a minimalist, diagrammatic visual language: clean shapes,
  restrained palette, systems legible at a glance.

## Format and scope

- One run: **60 minutes maximum**, with three or four meaningfully different
  sacrifice paths that invite a second run.
- **Compact 3D department** (a few rooms) plus a **desk/interface layer** where
  the survival system lives.
- **No cutscenes.** In-engine moments, emails, and short exchanges replace them.
- Fictional people and institutions. Leonardo's experiences are inspiration,
  never reproduced literally; no real person or institution is depicted.
- **Content level: mature themes, roughly 16+**, at the Level 2 darkness below.
  A content note appears at the start, and the most intense scenes can be
  softened without changing the story or the endings.

## Darkness (approved Level 2)

Dark in consequence and atmosphere, not in shock. Allowed: permanent
relationship rupture, complicity with named victims, implied substance use,
strong language, and institutional dread. Not allowed: graphic self-harm, gore,
or explicit abuse. The best outcome is dignity and connection, never a clean
win.

## Scientific field

Fictional **soil-microbiome research**. Invisible, unglamorous labour under the
ground that sustains everything above it is the metaphor for junior researchers.
The science stays plausible, legible, and non-actionable: no real protocol, no
treatment claim, and no unsupported real capability. The proposed
deep-biosphere late-game complication is not yet approved.

## Survival direction

Pressure comes from the PI's escalating and contradictory paper requests, a
fixed contract clock, and resources that do not stretch: **time, energy, and
department standing**, with a recurring pay-and-rent pressure event. Failure is
real and warned: an early ejection or a decision to quit is a reachable ending.

The exact core loop — the **Revision Spiral**: turns, request cards, moving
standard, stale evidence, complicity actions, and the review set piece — is
designed and approved in Block A2. Nothing in this document authorizes it.

## Non-negotiables

- The critique of academia and the sarcastic, caustic, absurdist tone.
- Recognition before instruction: the game must feel lived in.
- Never bland or boring: every scene is a decision, an escalation, or a joke.

## Explicit non-goals

- Bland, neutral, or toothless satire.
- Real people, institutions, or literal autobiography.
- Actionable wet-lab or field protocols, real treatment claims, or unsupported
  real capabilities.
- Horror gameplay, combat, chase sequences, or gore.
- Cutscenes or long passive sequences.
- A perfect ending or a power fantasy that fixes academia.
